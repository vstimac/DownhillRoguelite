# environment

Light and atmosphere driven by the run clock: the day/night cycle (sun, sky, ambient light, fog) and the weather (`weather/`).

- `WeatherSystem` publishes `GameEvents.CurrentWeather` every physics frame from the seed, the circle (RunState.Depth) and the clock, using `WeatherForecast` (stateless) and the profiles in `weather/default_weather.tres`.
- `DayNightCycle` and `WeatherParticles` show the weather.
- Gameplay systems read `GameEvents.CurrentWeather` themselves: grip and snow speed (PlayerController), wind (BalanceController), chill and heat (CarrierNeeds). See docs/survival-loop.md.
- Foliage and life (TerrainStreamer adds them): `FoliageWind` sets the shader globals the grass and trees sway by (wind direction, strength, gust noise, the carrier parting the grass; `--wind=0..1`, `--gale`); `FallingLeaves` drifts leaves round the camera where `Biome.LeafFall` says (thick in Greed; `--leaves` everywhere); `Fireflies` glow on dry nights where `Biome.Fireflies` says (Lust most; `--fireflies` everywhere, any hour). Look at the grass and trees in `debug/FoliageLab.tscn`.
- One style (`WorldStyle`, `world_natural.gdshader`): everything placed on the mountain is drawn from one muted, natural palette (stone, bark, wood, cloth, metal, foliage and a few flower and mushroom tints), each tint shaded by the terrain's own soil or rock texture, so props, set pieces, signs, stations and animals sit in the ground's light instead of their art pack's flat colours. `WorldStyle.Restyle` maps a model's materials onto the palette (Kenney names, Quaternius `MI_` atlases, colour-map kits by file name); `Tinted` gives a hand-built mesh its palette entry; `RestyleCreature` smooths an animal's facets and gives it a fur-grained coat. Anything new must use these (a raw `StandardMaterial3D` fails `debug/tests/WorldStyleTests.tscn`); glows, flags and other unshaded effects are exempt. `WorldStyle.CoatOf` is a muted coat colour (optionally spotted) for an animal: `WildlifeSpecies.CoatColours` recolours a cartoon model by surface (the snake is olive-brown), and the night beasts (`BeastSpecies` ModelStretch, CoatColour, CoatSpots, Mane*) are shaped from the Quaternius wolf rig — a long, low, spotted leopard and a heavy lion with a mane on the neck bone — since no cat model fits the style.
- Checking by eye: `debug/EnvironmentShots.tscn` photographs fixed spots down the route, `debug/CloseUpShots.tscn` every set piece, landmark, animal, night beast, station, wayside site, sign and lore mark up close beside the carrier (and prints each one's size and how far its foot is off the ground), and `debug/WalkShots.tscn` walks the carrier down the path of each circle, a frame a second. All three run windowed (`-- --shots-directory=...`).
- Solid things block: anything that looks solid and stands above a knee (`WorldStyle.KneeHeight`, 0.5 m) has a body — rocks, outcrops, walls, stumps and lost gear, bushes over 1.2 m (TerrainChunk's cover bodies), every set-piece part but vines (`SetPieceNode.Blocks`; arches, fences and big ruins collide with their own shape so their openings stay open), cairns, shrines and signposts. Grass, flowers, pebbles and small shrubs don't. `WorldStyleTests` walks a carrier-sized probe into samples of each kind.
