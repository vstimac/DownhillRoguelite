# Narrative: the endless descent

> **Story bible (2026-11-13):** `docs/STORY_BIBLE.md` is now the shared narrative baseline for
> every agent and writer. This file is the earlier setting (2026-11-06) and its Dante circle table
> still describes the game's stages. **Open conflict, awaiting the director:** the premise below
> ("you descend to become a god") answers a question the bible keeps OPEN ("why is descending
> necessary?", section 9). Until it is decided, don't build new content on the godhood premise and
> don't remove the existing godhood lines either.

Adopted 2026-11-06 from the director's concept. This gives the endless procedural descent its reason: the world is infinite because the pursuit is infinite.

> **You descend to become a god. The mountain never ends.**

## Premise

The carrier descends through an infinite underworld, believing that somewhere below lies godhood, the ultimate state of existence. The deeper they go, the closer it feels, but there is no final arrival.

## Pillars

1. **Infinite descent.** There is always another mountain, another valley, another circle beneath the current one.
2. **Illusion of progress.** The carrier grows stronger, better equipped, and survives increasingly impossible terrain, but godhood stays out of reach.
3. **Eternal ambition.** The wish to become something greater is what keeps the player descending.

## Never confirmed

The game never says whether godhood exists. It only hints:
- Ancient structures suggest earlier beings reached it.
- NPCs speak of those who descended further than anyone.
- Relics look increasingly divine the deeper they are found.
- The environment becomes less recognisable and more abstract with depth.
- Every milestone promises the next one will be different.

## The nine circles

The nine circles of Dante's *Inferno* (public domain, 14th century) are the first nine major stages, not nine levels with a final boss. After the ninth, which should feel like the end of the world, the descent continues into new layers.

Draw on the poem itself, not on later adaptations (games, films, art) of it.

Each circle's punishment can be a terrain and carrying rule built from systems that already exist:

| Circle | In the poem | Descent rule (proposal) | Built on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Limbo | Grey, quiet, without hope | Tutorial-gentle slopes, fog, first relics and NPCs | Locomotion |
| 2 Lust | Souls blown by an endless storm | Constant shifting wind pushing the load | Balance (new wind torque) |
| 3 Gluttony | Cold rain and filth | Mud and rain everywhere: low grip, long stops | Surfaces (mud) |
| 4 Greed | Rolling heavy weights | Contracts force heavier cargo; boulders roll downhill | Load mass, balance |
| 5 Wrath | Fighting in the river Styx | Swamp crossings, sinking footing | Surfaces, foot IK |
| 6 Heresy | Burning tombs | Heat damages fragile cargo; hot ground | Item tags, health |
| 7 Violence | River of boiling blood, burning sand | Damage terrain; scree and sand slides | Health, surfaces |
| 8 Fraud | Ten ditches of deceivers | Maze-like branching routes; false paths on the map | Map graph |
| 9 Treachery | A frozen lake | Ice: near-zero grip, falls everywhere | Surfaces (ice), ragdoll |
| 10+ | Beyond the poem | Abstract, unrecognisable layers mixing earlier rules | Depth scaling |

## Effect on the plan

- The plan's biome list (meadow → ridge → forest → scree → glacier) becomes the look *within* the circles, not the progression itself.
- **NPCs who talk about the deep** fit the NobodyWho addon (local LLM dialogue). Keep them in the world, not in flat menus: a voice and a 3D figure at a camp.
- Score stays "depth reached plus cargo delivered", framed as how close you came.
